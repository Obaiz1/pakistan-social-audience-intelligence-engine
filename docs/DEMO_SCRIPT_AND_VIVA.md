# Teacher demo script (5–8 minutes) and viva questions

Written for: the presenting student, to rehearse before the class demonstration.

## Demo script
Open the deployed app (URL in README). Keep the dark/light toggle and the dataset switcher in view.

1. **Overview (≈45 s).** Point at the striped banner: *SYNTHETIC DEMO — NOT REAL SOCIAL MEDIA OBSERVATIONS*, with post and comment counts and the snapshot date. Say: one category (women's lawn suits), six fictional brands, four platforms, comments in English, Roman Urdu and Urdu. Show the "Try this project" row — each card answers one assignment question.
2. **Q1 Content (≈1 min).** `/content`. Explain the attention index (1.00 = that brand's typical post on that platform) and why: brands and platforms differ in size, so raw likes would mislead. Point at the CI dot plot, switch to *Table* to show n, CI, Cliff's δ and BH-adjusted p. Mention that photos have no view counts, so they are excluded from the view-based ER panel, not given zero. Open one post in the explorer.
3. **Q2 Audience (≈1.5 min).** `/audience`. State the method in one sentence: comments are normalised (Urdu letter forms, Roman-Urdu spellings, PII removed), embedded with a multilingual sentence model, and grouped by KMeans chosen with a fixed rule (silhouette × stability, noise and minimum-size gates). Click a price-like cluster: show "Why this group exists" (terms, exemplars in different languages, name agreement %), the language bar, and campaign lift.
4. **Q3 Anomalies (≈1 min).** `/anomalies`. Filter "Above peers", open the top flag: value vs cohort median, P5–P95 range, robust z, cohort size, and the caveat that this is not a fraud claim. Optionally show a "need mix" flag (complaint storm).
5. **Q4 Needs (≈45 s).** Back on `/audience` (or `/campaigns`), scroll to *Needs by campaign*: ▲ marks needs ≥ 1.5× more common during that campaign (e.g. discount questions during sale campaigns, delivery during Eid).
6. **Q5 Recommendations (≈1 min).** `/recommendations`. Open a P1 card: what to do, why (with numbers), evidence chips (click one to jump to the cluster or anomaly), the suggested A/B or service test, and limitations.
7. **Data, exports, method (≈1 min).** `/data`: dataset provenance, quality checks, CSV template, upload preview with field mapping and validation (the public demo is read-only, so confirm is disabled — imports persist when run locally). `/reports`: download the PDF. `/methodology`: official title, candidate comparison table, independent audit, synthetic recovery and limitations.
8. **What was reused vs new (≈30 s).** From Project 1 (Content Detective): YouTube client, honest metrics and statistics, language ID. From SignalSpend: the Next.js + FastAPI Vercel layout and UI patterns. New for Project 3: comment clustering, need profiles, peer-cohort anomalies, and evidence-linked recommendations.

## Viva questions (with short answers)
1. **Clustering vs classification?** Classification needs labelled examples and predicts known classes; clustering groups unlabelled comments by similarity and lets the needs emerge. Names were attached *after* discovery, and we report how many members agree with each name.
2. **What is an embedding?** A vector representation of a sentence where similar meanings land close together. We use a multilingual model, so "Price kya hai?" and "What is the price?" can be neighbours.
3. **Why a multilingual sentence model and not just TF-IDF?** TF-IDF matches surface strings; it splits Urdu-script and Latin-script comments that mean the same thing. We keep TF-IDF + character n-grams as the explainable baseline and report it next to the semantic model.
4. **HDBSCAN vs KMeans?** HDBSCAN finds dense groups and labels sparse points as noise, with no k to choose; KMeans assigns every point to one of k groups. On this corpus, HDBSCAN either left most comments as noise or produced hundreds of tiny groups, failing our pre-declared gates, so KMeans with k chosen by silhouette × stability was selected.
5. **How was k chosen without forcing the rubric categories?** A fixed rule: among candidates with ≤ 35 % noise and every cluster ≥ 1 %, maximise cosine silhouette × stability (ARI across re-seeded fits). Agreement with the generator's hidden needs was reported but never used to choose.
6. **Why is sentiment not enough?** "Delivery kitne din mein hogi?" is neutral but a clear delivery need; "Price bohat zyada hai" is negative but a price need. Marketing actions depend on the need, not only the polarity.
7. **How do you detect anomalies fairly?** Compare each post only with comparable posts (same brand, platform and content type when there are ≥ 20; otherwise broader), using a robust z-score of log engagement (median/MAD) with |z| ≥ 3.5, plus a comments-per-like check, a need-mix test and Isolation Forest as a second opinion.
8. **Is a flagged post fraudulent?** No. It is statistically unusual relative to peers; causes include paid boosts, influencer reposts, giveaways or data errors. The app says so on every flag.
9. **How are missing values handled?** Null means "not reported" (e.g. YouTube shares, views on photos) and is never turned into zero; rates are only computed when their denominator exists, and weighted rates use Σ numerator ÷ Σ denominator.
10. **Why exclude posts younger than 7 days?** Their engagement is still accumulating; comparing them with mature posts would make new posts look weak.
11. **How are recommendations kept honest?** Each card is produced by a rule only when its evidence passes thresholds (support, agreement, significance after correction, effect size), and it lists the evidence IDs, sample size, limitations and a test. No LLM writes the content.
12. **Is the data real?** The classroom dataset is synthetic and labelled as such everywhere. An observed YouTube collector is implemented; it needs the owner's API key, and YouTube's policy means observed comment text is never redistributed and data expires after 30 days.
13. **Ethics and privacy?** Official APIs or authorised exports only, no usernames stored, PII stripped before modelling, comment text withheld when reuse is not permitted, source and permission recorded per dataset.
14. **Why Next.js + FastAPI on Vercel?** One URL, typed frontend, Python for the analytics; heavy ML runs offline and the deployed function serves precomputed results, keeping it fast and inside Vercel's limits.
15. **What would improve real-world validity?** A real, permitted comment corpus labelled by humans in all three languages, inter-annotator agreement, re-running the selection rule on it, and A/B tests to turn associations into causal evidence.
