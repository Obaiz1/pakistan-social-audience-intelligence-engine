# Methodology

*Project 3: Pakistan Social Audience & Campaign Intelligence Engine (Clustering Based).* The same content is served live at `/methodology` with the selected run's actual numbers.

## 1. Data
- **Unit of analysis.** A social **post** (brand, platform, campaign, content type, product category, likes, comments, shares, views, age) and its retained **comments** (text, language, reference topic, sentiment, purchase-intent hint). Schema: `docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md`.
- **Synthetic classroom corpus (shipped).** Seed 42, 3,000 posts over 12 months (15 Nov 2025 – 13 Nov 2026), six fictional brands, Instagram/TikTok/Facebook/YouTube. It simulates brand-owned analytics exports: shares exist where such exports report them; views are null for photos and graphic sale posts; YouTube has no shares. Engagement depends on brand scale, platform, content type, campaign and post age (saturating growth) with log-normal noise. Comment needs depend on content type, campaign, brand and platform (e.g. sale posts → discount questions, Eid → availability and delivery, one brand with delivery problems, one with sizing problems). Comment text is generated compositionally from multilingual templates with slot fillers, Roman-Urdu spelling variants, code-switching, emojis, greetings and noise. About 3 % of mature posts receive injected anomalies (viral spikes, engagement collapses, complaint surges, giveaway floods); the ground truth is stored separately and used only for evaluation.
- **Observed data (optional).** YouTube Data API v3 for official channels pinned by ID; videos become posts with `content_type = video`, campaigns inferred from title keywords, shares null. Comment text stays local; the shipped bundle keeps derived fields only and expires after 30 days.
- **Imports.** User CSVs go through header mapping, strict validation, PII-column removal, join checks and a source attestation; they are clustered with the lexical baseline.

## 2. Q1 — content performance
Per platform, `interactions = likes + comments (+ shares where the platform's data reports them for ≥ 95 % of its posts)`; `er_views = interactions / views` only where views exist. Because brands and platforms differ in scale, the **attention index** divides each post's interactions by the median of mature posts of the same brand × platform (1.00 = typical). Posts younger than 7 days are excluded from baselines and comparisons. Groups (content type, campaign, platform) are compared on mature posts with medians and bootstrap 95 % CIs; each group with n ≥ 20 is tested against all other posts (Mann-Whitney U, Cliff's δ), p-values are Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted, and Kruskal–Wallis is reported across groups. Weighted engagement rates are Σ interactions ÷ Σ views. All results are associational. (Statistics code reused from Project 1.)

## 3. Q2/Q4 — comment clustering
1. **Normalisation.** NFKC; Arabic letter forms folded to Urdu; diacritics removed; URLs, e-mails, @mentions and Pakistani mobile numbers replaced by placeholders; emojis mapped to tokens (`emoji_love`, …); Latin lower-cased with repeated letters squeezed; a short list of unambiguous Roman-Urdu/English spelling variants canonicalised. No stopword removal.
2. **Boilerplate removal (embedding input only).** A closed list of greetings, politeness words and placeholders is removed (ADR-011).
3. **Language ID.** Script ratio decides Urdu vs Latin vs mixed; Latin text is split into English and Roman Urdu by lexicon counts (adapted from Project 1).
4. **Representations.** *Semantic:* `paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2` sentence embeddings (768-d, L2-normalised). *Lexical baseline:* TF-IDF word 1–2-grams + character 2–5-grams → TruncatedSVD(100) → L2. *Hybrid:* 0.6·semantic ⊕ 0.4·lexical, re-normalised.
5. **Candidates.** KMeans (k ∈ {8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 28}, 10 inits, seed 42; fitted on unique texts weighted by frequency, which is the same objective) and HDBSCAN (min cluster size 60/120/250, min samples 10) on a 10-d UMAP projection (cosine, seed 42).
6. **Selection (ADR-007/007a).** Eligible: noise ≤ 35 %, every cluster ≥ 1 % of clustered comments, 2 ≤ k ≤ 40, and representation ∈ {semantic, hybrid}. Rank by cosine silhouette on a fixed 5,000-comment sample; for the top four compute stability (mean pairwise ARI over three seeds for KMeans, 80 % bootstraps for HDBSCAN); select the maximum of silhouette × stability. Lexical candidates are reported as the baseline.
7. **Naming and profiling.** Each comment also gets a rule-based *reference* need label (multilingual keyword lexicon). A cluster is named after the majority reference label if ≥ 45 % of members carry it, otherwise "Mixed: A / B"; the agreement share is shown everywhere. Profiles include size, share (denominator = clustered comments), cohesion (mean cosine to the centre), class-based TF-IDF top terms, language and need mix, up to six exemplars closest to the centre (at most two per language), date range and a generated explanation that warns when one language dominates.
8. **Evaluation (reported, not selected on).** Internal metrics for every candidate; agreement with the synthetic generator's hidden needs and languages (ARI/NMI/purity); an audit on 100 independently written, hand-labelled multilingual comments assigned to the nearest cluster centre; language-ID and rule-labeller accuracy. See `docs/CLUSTERING_EVALUATION.md`.

## 4. Q3 — unusual posts
Peer-cohort robust z-scores on log engagement and log comments-per-like, an exact binomial test of the most over-represented discovered need per post, and Isolation Forest as a secondary score. Details and validation: `docs/ANOMALY_METHOD.md`.

## 5. Q5 — recommendations
Cards are emitted only when evidence passes explicit gates: need clusters (agreement ≥ 50 %, ≥ 30 comments, ≥ 4 % share); need concentrations by campaign/brand/platform/content type (lift ≥ 1.5, ≥ 50 comments, two-proportion z-test, BH p < 0.05); content effects (BH p < 0.05 and effect ≥ small); recurring anomalies (≥ 3 flags). Each card states what to do, why (with numbers), linked evidence IDs, where, priority, evidence strength, sample size, metrics, limitations and a test plan. Optional LLM wording (local only) may only rephrase, and any rewrite introducing a number absent from the card is rejected (Project 1's number lock).

## 6. Reproducibility
Every result carries `dataset_id`, `run_id`, `model_version`, `sample_size`, `generated_at` and assumptions. The bundle's `dataset.json` holds SHA-256 hashes of every table; CI re-verifies them. Commands: README → *Local setup* and *Tests*.

## 7. Limitations
Synthetic text is easier and more regular than real comments, so clustering and anomaly scores on it overstate real-world performance; the audit set is written by the same author as the templates (not fully independent); the rule labeller names clusters and can be wrong for sarcasm or heavy code-switching; observed YouTube data was not collected in this build; Instagram/TikTok/Facebook are supported only through authorised CSV exports; all comparisons are associational.
